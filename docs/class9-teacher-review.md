# Class 9 — points for teachers to check

Every Class 9 lesson was written to the chapter-kit standard and then checked by an
independent AI reviewer. The reviewer recomputed every answer, ran the Java and Python
code, and balanced every equation. The points below are the ones the writers and
reviewers were **not fully sure of**. None is known to be wrong; each needs a teacher's
yes or no against the current textbook.

Boards: CBSE (NCERT, rationalised), ICSE (CISCE), Karnataka State Board (KSEAB / KTBS).

## Syllabus and chapter names (Karnataka especially)
- **Chapter lists:** they were made from known syllabi, not the printed books. Please
  compare each board's Class 9 list with this year's syllabus.
- **Karnataka Class 9 maths and science:** the list follows the older (pre-rationalised)
  NCERT book. It still has Constructions, Areas of Parallelograms and Triangles,
  Probability, Why Do We Fall Ill? and Natural Resources. The lessons assume Karnataka
  also keeps:
  - the remainder theorem;
  - graphs of linear equations;
  - Heron's formula for quadrilaterals;
  - cuboid, cube and cylinder;
  - separating mixtures;
  - the mole.

  Please confirm against the KTBS book. In the shared chapters, the new Class 9 lessons
  replaced the older general lessons. So if Karnataka still teaches separation methods
  or the mole, those lessons need linking back.
- **Karnataka social science titles:** these are grouped summaries and may not be the
  KTBS chapter names.
  - **Geography:** the KTBS book probably has more, shorter chapters on soils, forests,
    power, tourism and so on.
  - **Economics:** the KTBS book may split Natural Resources and Human Resources.
    "Meaning and Concepts of Economics" may be a Class 8 chapter.
  - **History:** Medieval Europe and Modern Europe may be separate chapters. "The Age of
    Revolutions" may be printed as "Era of Revolutions".
  - **Political science:** "Elections and Political Parties" may be one chapter or two.
  - **Sociology:** the second chapter may be called "Social Groups".
  - **Business studies:** check that both chapters are Class 9.
- **CBSE computers:** the chapters now follow the IT (402) Part B units. Cyber Safety and
  Getting Started with Python are extra chapters; Python belongs to AI (417).
- **Content NCERT may have cut in the rationalised book:**
  - **Taught anyway, with a board note:** triangle inequalities, the Constructions
    chapter, the angle-sum proof, Heron's formula for quadrilaterals, the separation
    methods and the mole.
  - **Kept, possibly beyond the current book:** conservation of momentum, relative
    density, reverberation, SONAR and the ear, the Java section of *Forest Society*, the
    Maasai section of *Pastoralists*, population composition, and wildlife
    conservation.
- **ICSE scope:**
  - **Maths:**
    - Is mode in ICSE 9 Statistics?
    - Are complementary angles in Trigonometry?
    - Compound interest is taught with half-yearly compounding and finding P, r or n.
    - Co-ordinate geometry covers the graphical solution and the distance formula.
  - **Physics:**
    - Spherical mirrors are taught as the kind of image only. Are they in ICSE 9?
    - Is conduction, convection and radiation in the Heat and Energy chapter?
    - Echo may belong to Class 10.
  - **Chemistry:** the covalent-molecule list and the pollution treaties (Paris,
    Montreal).
  - **Biology:** nutrition and teeth are covered under Human Anatomy and Physiology.
  - **Geography:** Weathering and Denudation covers only running water, with no wind,
    glaciers or waves.
  - **History:** is the Sangam Age still listed?
- **Pastoralists in the Modern World:** CBSE may assess it only in periodic tests.

## Maths
- **Statistics:** CBSE rationalised Class 9 keeps graphs only, and mean, median and mode
  were removed (board notes say so).
- **Numbers and Pythagoras:** NCERT 9 states that √2 is irrational without a proof; ICSE
  asks for the proof. Only √2 and √3 are placed on the number line. The Pythagoras proof
  uses similar triangles, which CBSE meets in Class 10.
- **Memory aid:** the made-up aid "SOAP" is used for the signs in a³ ± b³.

## Science
- **Physics figures:**
  - G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ (NCERT gives 6.673).
  - Speed of sound: 331 m/s at 0 °C and 344 m/s at 22 °C.
  - The NCERT speed table at 25 °C.
  - An echo needs 0.1 s, so the minimum distance is 17.2 m.
- **Screw gauge:** the zero-error sign follows the Selina convention.
- **ICSE physics values:**
  - atmospheric pressure 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa;
  - ice 0.92 g/cm³;
  - water expands about 9% on freezing;
  - sea water 1030 kg/m³.
- **Chemistry figures:**
  - colloid particles about 1–100 nm;
  - about 1 in 12,000 alpha particles bounce back;
  - the nucleus is about 10⁵ times smaller than the atom;
  - Thomson's model 1904, Mendeleev 1869, Moseley 1913, Boyle 1662.
- **ICSE chemistry:** acetanilide is the negative-catalyst example; N₂ + O₂ react at
  about 3000 °C; the ozone layer is about 15–35 km up.
- **Biology:**
  - the mouth and oesophagus lining is left out of the squamous epithelium row (NCERT
    puts it there);
  - cardiac muscle is described as striated;
  - lung volumes (tidal 500 mL, inspiratory reserve 2500–3000 mL, expiratory reserve
    about 1000 mL, residual 1500 mL);
  - energy values 4 / 4 / 9 kcal per gram;
  - germination examples (onion as an epigeal monocot);
  - *Helicobacter pylori*, Nobel Prize 2005.

## History
- **Ancient India:** where textbooks disagree, dates say "about" and give both versions:
  - Harappan mature phase 2600–1900 or 2500–1750 BCE;
  - Mahavira 540–468 or 599–527 BCE;
  - Ashoka about 273–232 BCE;
  - Kalinga War about 261 BCE;
  - Sangam Age about 300 BCE – 300 CE.

  Also check:
  - the lands Seleucus gave up (Herat is debated);
  - Ajanta credited to the Vakatakas;
  - Rock Edict XII (tolerance) and XIII (Kalinga).
- **Medieval and Karnataka:**
  - Bengaluru bought by Chikkadevaraja Wodeyar in 1689;
  - Keladi founded around 1500 (some books say 1499);
  - Basavappa Nayaka as author of *Shivatattva Ratnakara*;
  - Kanteerayi hana coins;
  - the Dilwara temples (the later one is Vaghela);
  - the KRS dam (begun 1911);
  - Torna captured in 1646 (some books say 1645).
- **World history:**
  - The Enabling Act is given as "March 1933" (NCERT prints 3 March; it was passed on
    23 March).
  - First Five Year Plan 1927–32, as in NCERT.
  - Napoleon brought back slavery "about ten years later" (1802).
  - Spinning jenny 1764; Jesuits 1534 (approved by the Pope in 1540).
  - Versailles losses: 13% of territory, 75% of iron, 26% of coal.
  - Nazi vote share: 2.6% in 1928, 37% in 1932.

- **Economics:**

- **Sociology:** the examples are Kamala and Amala (1920), Cooley and Sumner, and the
  Soligas.

## Languages and computers
- **English:** word limits of 100–120 words for a descriptive paragraph and 150–200 for a
  story.
- **Hindi:**
  - the उपसर्ग 'अ' is left out because books disagree;
  - नुक्ता is used on borrowed words (ख़रीदी);
  - "मत" sentences are left out;
  - संदेहवाचक: "शायद राधा गाना गाती है" or गाती हो / गाती होगी?
- **Java:**
  - all 24 programs compile and give the stated output;
  - the JVM is described as an interpreter, with no JIT;
  - the switch types listed are int, char, byte, short and String.
- **Python:** the interpreter is described as stopping "at the first error line" (a
  simplification).
- **Cyber safety:** the 1930 helpline and cybercrime.gov.in.
